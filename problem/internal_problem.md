# When You Are The Problem

Applying the four-part problem framework to yourself means shifting the variables from external constraints to internal behaviors. When you are the bottleneck, the entire system is self-influenced.

### 1. Trigger (The Root Cause)
The trigger is *you*—but more specifically, it is the exact behavior, mindset, or blind spot generating the friction. It asks: *What action or part of me is the actual cause of this experience?* By identifying your own actions as the trigger and the origin point of the problem, you reclaim the autonomy to change the outcome.

### 2. Context (Self-Imposed Rules)
When you are the problem, the context isn't an external environment; it's your own mental baseline. It is the habits, assumptions, and personal narratives you carry into a situation. You set the rules of the game, often without realizing it, establishing a status quo built on your own limitations or comfort zones.

### 3. Conflict (Internal Friction)
This is self-sabotage, cognitive dissonance, or ego colliding with reality. The opposing forces are internal: your ambition fighting your procrastination, your desire for control fighting your need to collaborate, or your expectations failing to match your actual effort. You are the friction.

### 4. Impact (Self-Inflicted Damage)
The stakes are entirely personal. The impact is the burnout, the stalled projects, the isolated relationships, or the mental fatigue you bring upon yourself. Because the damage is self-inflicted, it is often easier to ignore or rationalize away, which makes defining the impact crucial to forcing actual change.
